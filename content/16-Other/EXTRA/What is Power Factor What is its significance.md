## Power Factor

Power Factor is the ratio of useful (active) power to the total (apparent) power supplied in an AC circuit.

$$PF = \cos\phi = \frac{P}{S} = \frac{\text{Active Power (W)}}{\text{Apparent Power (VA)}}$$

The power triangle relates the three types of power:

S² = P² + Q²

- P = Active Power (Watts) — does actual work
- Q = Reactive Power (VAR) — does no useful work
- S = Apparent Power (VA) — total power supplied

PF ranges from 0 to 1. A PF of 1 is ideal.

---

### Significance

1. **Productivity** — A higher PF means more of the supplied power is doing useful work. A low PF wastes power as reactive power Q.

2. **Current demand** — Low PF draws more current from the source for the same useful output, increasing losses.

3. **Transmission losses** — Higher current means more heat loss in cables and equipment (I²R losses).

4. **Cost** — Power companies penalize consumers with low PF since it burdens the supply system without useful return.
