## Power Triangle

The Power Triangle shows the relationship between the three types of power in an AC circuit.

---

### The Triangle

$$S^2 = P^2 + Q^2$$
- The **vertical side** = Q (Reactive Power)
- The **horizontal side** = P (Active Power)
- The **hypotenuse** = S (Apparent Power)
- The **angle φ** = phase angle between V and I

---

### The Three Powers

| Symbol | Name           | Unit             | Meaning                             |
| ------ | -------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| P      | Active Power   | Watt (W)         | Actually does useful work           |
| Q      | Reactive Power | VAR              | Goes back and forth, no useful work |
| S      | Apparent Power | Volt-Ampere (VA) | Total power the source supplies     |

---

### Definitions

**P — Active Power (Watts)**
The power that does real work — runs motors, lights bulbs, heats elements.
This is what you pay for on your electricity bill.

**Q — Reactive Power (VAR)**
Power that bounces between source and load — needed by coils and capacitors to build magnetic and electric fields. Does no useful work.

**S — Apparent Power (VA)**
The total power the supply must provide. Combination of P and Q.
That is why generators and transformers are rated in VA, not Watts.

**φ (phi) — Phase Angle**
The angle between Voltage and Current.
Bigger angle = more reactive power = less useful work.

**Power Factor = cos φ**
How efficiently power is being used.
- PF = 1 → perfect, all power is useful
- PF = 0.5 → half the power is wasted

---

### Formulas

$$S = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2}$$

$$P = S \cos\phi$$

$$Q = S \sin\phi$$

$$PF = \cos\phi = \frac{P}{S}$$

---

### Memory Trick

> Like carrying a bag at an angle —
> **S** = total effort, **P** = forward progress, **Q** = sideways wasted effort.
