
## Transformer vs Induction Motor

| Parameter | Transformer | Induction Motor |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Purpose** | Transfers electrical energy between circuits | Converts electrical energy to mechanical energy |
| **Moving Parts** | None (static device) | Rotor rotates (moving parts present) |
| **Input / Output** | Electrical → Electrical | Electrical → Mechanical |
| **Frequency** | Output frequency = Input frequency | Output is rotation (no frequency) |
| **Working Principle** | Mutual Induction | Electromagnetic Induction + Rotating Magnetic Field |
| **Windings** | Primary & Secondary windings | Stator winding & Rotor bars |
| **Slip** | No concept of slip | Slip is essential for torque production |
| **Efficiency** | Very high (~95–99%) | Moderate (~85–95%) |
| **Cooling** | Oil or air cooled | Air cooled (fan on rotor) |
| **Applications** | Power transmission, voltage step-up/down | Fans, pumps, compressors, conveyor belts |

---

### Key Differences Explained

**1. Energy Conversion**
- Transformer: Electrical → Electrical (only voltage/current levels change)
- Induction Motor: Electrical → Mechanical (produces rotation)

**2. Moving Parts**
- Transformer has **no moving parts** — it is a static device.
- Induction Motor has a **rotating rotor** — it is a dynamic device.

**3. Slip**
- In a Transformer, primary and secondary are magnetically coupled with no slip.
- In an Induction Motor, the rotor always runs **slower than the rotating magnetic field** — this difference is called **slip** and is necessary to produce torque.

> **Slip (s) = (Ns - Nr) / Ns**
> where Ns = Synchronous speed, Nr = Rotor speed

**4. Working Principle**
- Transformer works on **Faraday's Law of Mutual Induction**.
- Induction Motor works on **rotating magnetic field (RMF)** created by 3-phase AC supply.
